you are a hiring manager tasked with hiring an intermediate full-stack software. what would you look for in their resume?

As a hiring manager looking for an intermediate full-stack software developer, I am scanning resumes for someone who has moved past the "hand-holding" junior phase but isn't yet expected to architect massive, complex systems from scratch. An intermediate developer should be an autonomous feature-builder who understands how the front-end, back-end, and database connect.
When I spend 10 seconds skimming a resume, here is exactly what I am looking for:

1. Proven Autonomy & Ownership (3–5 Years of Experience)

• The Baseline: I look for roughly 3 to 5 years of commercial experience. This sweet spot usually indicates they have seen code go to production, break, and need fixing.
• Ownership Metrics: Juniors list tasks ("Responsible for writing APIs"). I want to see impact. I look for bullets starting with strong action verbs that show they owned a feature from end to end:
• “Designed and implemented a new checkout service using Node.js and PostgreSQL, reducing API latency by 15%.”

2. T-Shaped Technical Competency

A good full-stack developer is a generalist, but an intermediate should have a "spine" of deep competence in at least one area.
• The Connected Stack: I look for a cohesive stack (e.g., the MERN stack, .NET + Angular, or Python + React) rather than a chaotic laundry list of 30 buzzwords.
• Back-End Solidity: Anyone can learn basic CSS or map an API response to a front-end component. I check if they understand database optimization (indexing, query profiling), data integrity, and building RESTful or GraphQL APIs.
• Front-End Fluency: They should show familiarity with modern state management, component lifecycle, and modern styling/frameworks (like React, Next.js, or Angular).

3. "Cloud-Aware" and DevOps Mindset

An intermediate developer shouldn't say, "It works on my machine." I want to see that they know how their code actually gets to the user.
• CI/CD & Git: I look for signs that they understand branch management, code review culture, and automated testing.
• Cloud Ecosystems: Mentioning experience deploying or managing basic cloud services (like AWS Lambda/EC2, Azure, or Google Cloud) or containerization tools like Docker immediately sets them apart.

4. Code Quality & Mentorship Potential

Because intermediates sit right in the middle of a engineering team, they are the glue.
• Testing: I deliberately look for the words "unit testing" or frameworks like Jest, Cypress, or Mocha. It shows they care about code quality.
• Collaboration: I love seeing mentions of participating in code reviews, writing technical documentation, or occasionally helping onboard junior team members. It proves they are scaling their own impact.

5. Format & Professionalism Red Flags

• The 1-2 Page Rule: If an intermediate developer has a 4-page resume, it tells me they cannot prioritize information. Keep it tight and impact-focused.
• Clean Layout: I prefer a clean, single-column markdown or simple PDF layout over over-styled, multi-column templates that try to mask a lack of experience or confuse applicant tracking systems (ATS).
